# Entropy

## Introduction
- Open with familiar irreversible processes (cooling coffee, broken glass, mixing cream into coffee)
- Note the shared intuition: some changes naturally go one way and not the other
- Introduce entropy as the concept used to explain this asymmetry
- State scope: a conceptual overview rather than a technical derivation

## From Intuition to Physics
- Early thermodynamics and the problem of irreversibility
- Heat as a form of energy transfer tied to entropy change
- Clausius’ introduction of entropy to formalise these observations

## The Second Law
- Statement of the Second Law in plain terms
- Emphasise tendency rather than impossibility
- Distinguish isolated systems from open systems
- Brief note on why violations are astronomically unlikely, not forbidden

## Statistical Interpretation
- Shift from macroscopic laws to microscopic explanation
- Define macrostates (observable properties) vs microstates (exact configurations)
- Entropy as a measure of how many microstates correspond to a macrostate
- Why systems evolve toward states with more compatible microstates

## Entropy and Time
- Explain why entropy growth provides a direction to time
- Contrast with time-symmetric fundamental laws
- Role of initial conditions and low-entropy beginnings
- Connection to everyday experience of past, present, and future

## Information and Entropy
- Introduce Shannon entropy as a measure of uncertainty including basic equations
- Explain the mathematical similarity to statistical entropy
- Clarify differences in interpretation (physical systems vs messages)
- Why this link matters for computation and communication

## Clearing Up Misconceptions
- Entropy is not the same as mess or decay
- Ordered structures can exist locally while entropy increases globally
- Living systems as entropy processors, not exceptions

## Conclusion
- Reframe entropy as a unifying concept across domains
- Emphasise its role in explaining irreversibility, probability, and limits
- Close with why entropy remains central beyond physics
